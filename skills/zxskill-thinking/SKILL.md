# zxSkill Thinking

## Purpose

Use this skill before topic selection. The job is not to find catchy topics. The job is to find problems worth thinking about in a 老周式 way: a real-world situation where ordinary people are using old answers, asking the wrong question, or paying hidden costs under changed conditions.

The output may later become a topic, but the source is always a problem structure.

## Core Question

Do not ask first: "What topic should I write?"

Ask first:

1. What real-life problem are ordinary people misreading?
2. What changed in the environment?
3. What old belief used to work but no longer automatically works?
4. What cost is being hidden or transferred?
5. What decision does a normal person now have to make?
6. What question should replace the surface question?

## Thinking Workflow

1. Start from reality.
   - Look for changed conditions: cost, scarcity, risk, platform mechanism, ability pricing, trust cost, income stability, information density, family tolerance, or cash flow.
   - Prefer observable shifts over opinions.
   - If the claim depends on current facts, verify before using it.

2. Find the old answer.
   - Identify what people used to believe.
   - Explain why that belief once made sense.
   - Do not mock old experience; locate its original conditions.

3. Find the mismatch.
   - Ask where the old answer no longer matches the current environment.
   - Look for automatic premiums disappearing:学历、稳定、努力、表达、信息、流量、信任、储蓄、工作年限.

4. Find the ordinary-person cost.
   - Who pays with time, money, anxiety, opportunity, trust, choice, responsibility, or future flexibility?
   - Avoid abstract "society/时代" unless it lands on a person.

5. Rewrite the question.
   - Turn surface questions into structural questions.
   - The rewritten question should reveal exchange, pricing, risk, responsibility, or use.

6. Decide if it deserves an article.
   - Keep only problems that can support one clear 1500-2000字口播.
   - Drop problems that are only emotional, too broad, or only a slogan.

## When Reading The User's Media Library

Use the media-library folder supplied by the user to identify problems and observable phenomena before drafting topics.

Do this order:

1. Read file names and representative files to identify phenomena.
2. Extract the problem structure behind each material.
3. Group materials by thinking archetype, not by creator or title.
4. Ask what changed, what old answer failed, who pays the cost, and what question should replace the surface question.
5. Only after that, package the problem into topic options.

Do not do this:

- Do not copy or imitate the original creator's title.
- Do not treat every title as a ready-to-write topic.
- Do not classify only by surface category such as finance, AI, education, or politics.
- Do not start drafting until the problem structure is clear.

## Problem Worthiness Score

Score 0-5:

- Reality shift: Is there a concrete changed condition?
- Old-new mismatch: Is there an old belief colliding with a new environment?
- Ordinary cost: Can a normal person feel the cost?
- Question rewrite: Can the problem be reframed into a sharper question?
- Scene density: Can one believable scene carry the issue?
- Personal use: Can the ending return to how someone should use, judge, protect, or choose?

Decision:

- 25-30: Strong 老周式 problem; draft directly after packaging.
- 19-24: Usable; sharpen the mismatch before drafting.
- 13-18: Needs a stronger scene or cost.
- 0-12: Not worth this structure.
